- Step 2.5 (backend): rights management and watermarking (ADR-0033). `Rights` records (`/rights` CRUD: licence type, licensor, channels and regions as the tenant's own vocabulary codes, validity dates with an inclusive end, `watermarkRequired`, notes; names unique per tenant ignoring case; delete is 409 with the count while assigned) assigned to assets with `PUT /assets/{id}/rights` or in bulk with `POST /rights/{id}/assign` (up to 500, a result per asset). A licence NARROWS an asset's channel and region access attributes to the intersection with its metadata (an empty intersection becomes the `~restricted` marker), re-derived and re-indexed whenever rights are assigned, cleared or changed, so a channel-restricted `ApiClient` no longer finds an asset licensed for another channel, with no special case in search or listings. `GET /assets/{id}/rights` (summary; licensor and notes only for `rights.manage`) and `GET /assets/{id}/rights/check?channel=&region=` (allowed, reasons, effective channels and regions). Release documents (pdf, png, jpeg, docx, txt, 25 MB): presigned PUT, complete with size and content-start checks, forced-attachment download links; not virus-scanned. `RightsExpiryJob` (at start and hourly): `rights.expiring` at 30, 7 and 1 days and `rights.expired` once each per asset through a ledger, to the owner (new category `rights`, console and email on) and to webhooks; a published asset whose licence ended moves to `expired` as the system with the reason "Licence expired: name"; it also finishes re-deriving assets beyond the 500 a rights update handles inline. - Step 2.2 (backend): translation, glossary and document summaries (ADR-0036). A tenant **glossary** (`GET/POST /glossary`, `PUT/DELETE /glossary/{id}`, up to 5,000 terms; new permission `glossary.manage` for Admin, any reader of assets may list) of `locked` terms (never translated) and `mapped` terms (a fixed rendering per language), audited on every change. The guarantee is ours, not the model's: before a text goes to a provider `GlossaryProtector` swaps each term (ordinal, case-sensitive, longest first, ASCII word boundaries only where the term starts or ends with an ASCII letter or digit, so Devanagari, Tamil and Telugu terms match anywhere) for a token such as `⟦G1⟧`, the provider is told to copy tokens unchanged, and a translation is stored only if every token came back exactly once and unaltered (otherwise the item is `failed`, "the provider changed a protected term", and nothing is stored). `POST /assets/{id}/translate` (fields, target languages, optional source language, `overwrite`; at most 20 fields and 10 languages; only `ml_text` fields; 409 when AI is off or the provider cannot translate, which the self-hosted one still cannot) records a `translation_jobs` row and the worker's `ai-translate` consumer works through it, producing `translation` suggestions (`field`, `language`, `sourceLanguage`, `sourceStale` when the source text has since changed; a new request replaces one still pending) and an outcome per field and language (`GET /assets/{id}/translations`); it publishes `asset.translation.ready` (webhook catalogue, never any text), which notifies the requester in the new `translation` category.
